Plenty of cat owners have bought a scratching post, watched their cat sniff it once, and then watched that same cat walk straight back to the sofa. It's easy to conclude that your cat "just doesn't like posts." Knowing how to choose a scratching post — and where to put it — is what turns that around.

Almost always, the real story is simpler: it was the wrong post, in the wrong place. Cats are picky about scratching surfaces for good reasons — and once you know what they're looking for, picking a winner gets a lot easier.

## What does your cat want from a scratching surface?

Think about why the couch is so popular. It's tall, it's heavy, and the fabric gives claws something to grab. A good post copies all three.

### Height: tall enough for a full stretch

Scratching is a stretch as much as anything else. A post needs to be tall enough for your cat to reach all the way up with their back fully extended — for most adult cats that means **around 30 inches or taller**. The short posts sold in a lot of stores are fine for kittens, but an adult cat has to hunch to use them, so they don't bother.

### Stability: no wobble, ever

This is the quiet deal-breaker. A cat puts real weight into a scratch, and a post that rocks or tips — even once — can put a cat off for good. Look for a **wide, heavy base**. If you already own a wobbly post, try wedging it into a corner or against a wall so it can't move.

### Material: something to sink claws into

**Sisal** — either rope or woven fabric — is the classic for a reason: it's rough, it shreds satisfyingly, and it lasts. Many cats also love plain **corrugated cardboard**, which is inexpensive and easy to replace. Carpet-covered posts are a mixed bag: some cats enjoy them, but loops can snag claws, and they can blur the line between "scratch this" and "scratch the rug."

### Direction: vertical, horizontal, or both

Watch where your cat scratches now. A cat who goes for sofa arms and door frames is a **vertical** scratcher and wants a post. A cat who works on rugs and carpet is a **horizontal** scratcher and will be happier with a flat or angled cardboard pad. Lots of cats like both, and a mix costs very little.

## Location matters as much as the post

A perfect post in the basement is still a post nobody uses. Cats scratch where life happens:

- **Right next to the furniture they already scratch.** This is the big one. You're offering an alternative, so put it where the decision gets made.
- **Near favorite sleeping spots.** Cats love a good scratch-and-stretch right after waking up.
- **In the rooms your family actually uses.** Scratching is partly a way of marking shared territory, so a post in the living room beats a post in the laundry room every time.

You can always move the post later. Once it's the clear favorite, shift it a few feet at a time toward where you'd prefer it to live.

## Make the introduction easy

- **Rub a little catnip or silvervine** into the post, or drag a wand toy up it so your cat's claws sink in mid-pounce. One good grab is often all it takes.
- **Praise and treat** when your cat uses it. Keep it cheerful and low-key.
- **Don't place your cat's paws on the post** and move them for them. Most cats dislike being handled that way, and it can turn them off the post entirely.
- **Don't throw out a shredded post too quickly.** To your cat, a ragged, scent-marked post is a well-loved one. If it needs replacing, keep the old one beside the new one for a week or two.
