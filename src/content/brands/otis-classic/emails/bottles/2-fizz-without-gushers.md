Carbonation means pressure. That's what gives you fizz, and it's also why an over-fermented bottle can gush. Five habits keep it under control:

1. **Set a reminder.** Once the bottles are fizzy, they belong in the fridge. Forgotten bottles are where most gushers come from.
2. **Watch the fast fruits.** Mango, pineapple and ginger ferment quickly. Check those batches at the two-day mark, or sooner in a warm kitchen.
3. **Burp if you're unsure.** Cracking the lid briefly once a day releases extra pressure. You'll give up a little fizz for peace of mind.
4. **Chill before opening, and open over the sink.** Cold kombucha holds its carbonation far more calmly than warm. Keep a hand on the cap and release it slowly.
5. **Keep fermenting bottles contained,** in a cabinet or a cooler, especially for your first few batches.

And use the right bottle. Second fermentation calls for thick, round glass made to hold carbonation. Check each bottle for chips or cracks before you fill it.

### Flat instead?

The most common cause is a tired gasket. After that, look at a cool room (give it a warmer spot or another day), too little fruit or juice, or too much headspace. Fresh ginger is a reliable fizz-builder.
